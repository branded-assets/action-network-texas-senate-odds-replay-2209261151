Reading this chart. Probability = Polymarket's book-derived price for each outcome token, sampled every 5 minutes. Dots on the line are individual filled trades at their actual execution price, which can print away from the prevailing quote. Volume bars are traded size per outcome token, in 3-hour buckets — shown separately rather than summed, since Democrat and Republican fills are complementary positions, not one economic event double-counted. The donut mirrors the leading side's current share of implied probability; it updates with the same replay position as everything else on the page.

What happened in these ten days. The market opened the window at 51.5% and sagged to 47.0% on 13 September — the single heaviest trading day in the window, $18.8k across 118 fills. From there it climbed almost without interruption: 52.5% by the close of 14 September, 59.5% by 18 September, and a 62.5% peak late on 21 September, on a second heavy day of $18.7k. It sits at 61.5% as this was built. Over the whole window $79k changed hands across 585 fills. The replay is where that repricing is legible: the climb is not one dramatic jump but a series of steady bids, which is what a market re-rating a fundamental looks like rather than one reacting to a headline.

On the two sides. The contract is literally a Yes/No on "Will the Democrats win the Texas Senate race in 2026?", so the red line is strictly not-Democrat rather than Republican as such. In practice the distinction is academic here: Polymarket runs a separate Republican contract on the same race, and across 670 overlapping hourly observations the two prices summed to a mean of 0.9991 (min 0.980, max 1.020). They are pricing the same binary.
